# Calibration card: how often each parser is right at each confidence level

2026-10-31. Generated by `scripts/calibration_card.py`; aggregates only (no message text). For a layer whose job
is to ask when unsure, the confidence has to mean something: outputs at >= 0.9 should be right almost always, and
outputs below 0.6 are the ones it asks about.

Sources: MASSIVE hi-IN (public; "kal" direction and clock times, gold from the English parallel) and card-driven
typed messages written by volunteers (private; gold from the scenario cards, see RESULTS R16, R17). "Development" is
what the parsers were tuned on (MASSIVE train partition, typed batch 1); "held-out" is MASSIVE test and typed batch 2.

## Reading it

- **The sure band can be trusted.** Outputs at >= 0.9 are right 97-100% of the time for clock times, amounts, dates
  and "kal" on the held-out data (quantity is lower only because the scorer counts a correct quantity that is not on
  the card, such as "2 packets", as wrong; read it as a floor).
- **Below 0.6 the parser is asking, and it is right about as often as the prior.** "kal" is tomorrow about 90% of
  the time, so even its low-confidence guess is mostly right; the point is that it does not claim to know.
- **One miscalibration was found and fixed.** Unmarked amounts in a message with several numbers sat at 0.8 but
  were right only about half the time (50% in development, 48% held-out). They now report 0.55 and ask. The change
  was justified from development data; the held-out column above is after the fix, and nothing else was tuned on it.
- **Limits.** Card-driven writing, a handful of writers, one reference date, and the gold only covers amounts,
  quantities, dates and clock times. It says how reliable the parsers are on what people typed, not how often a
  model fails without them (that is H9, H10).
